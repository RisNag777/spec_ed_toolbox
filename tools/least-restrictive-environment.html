Least Restrictive Environment (LRE)

The first (default) option for teaching disabled children should be to teach them alongside their non-disabled peers. For this, classrooms need to be made more inclusive. A framework that a teacher can use is Multi-Tiered Systems of Support (MTSS), where different interventions are provided to different students over time and their responses to different interventions are observed before referring to special education.

  • A teacher would start with Tier 1 (Universal) support that could be beneficial to all children in the classroom regardless of whether they have a disability or not. This could be something like weekly reading comprehension checks, or re-explaining math concepts to the whole class.
  • If the students need more support, then the teacher can do Tier 2 support (for small groups of students), this still does not necessarily single out disabled students but could still be inclusive, for example, offering optional “concept check” sessions during lunch for any student who scored below 70%.
  • If the student needs additional support, then the teacher can go for Tier 3 (Individualized) support, where the student still attends classes with non-disabled peers but gets additional support/instruction from paraprofessionals or instructional assistants.
  • If all of these tiers are given a fair try and the student is still struggling, then and only then can they be referred to special education.
