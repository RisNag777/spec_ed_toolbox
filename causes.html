Potential causes and factors of disproportionality

  • Disproportionality stems from an unreasonably large number of students of color, or from low-income backgrounds being labeled as disabled. This is prevalent because of misunderstandings of students from different cultural backgrounds. For example, an MLL student who doesn’t know English might not grasp the peculiarities of English grammar very quickly. An observer who is specialized in special education might think this is a sign of disability, when in reality, it is a sign of a child struggling with a foreign language.
  • It used to be thought that a large share of low-income students were classed as disabled. However, this does not explain why so many more students of color are labeled as disabled over white students. Especially considering ‘soft disabilities’ like learning disabilities or intellectual disabilities which, unlike disabilities like blindness or deafness, are much more subjective and therefore, much more prone to bias. Low income students tend to have higher disability rates due to healthcare access, lead exposure, low birth weight, etc.
  • These students were much more likely to be separated from non-disabled peers and placed in separate classrooms. Children in separate special education classrooms are less likely to graduate high school, do well on achievement tests, and enroll in college.
